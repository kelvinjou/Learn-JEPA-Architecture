JEPA from Scratch

Four things to poke at

Companions to JEPA_from_scratch.pdf. Everything runs locally in this page — no server, no network, no build step. Open any file directly from disk.

Chapter 8

Multi-block masking

Best strategy 54.2, worst 15.5, on the paper's own ablation. Drag the scale and aspect-ratio ranges, then find out why the paper's "average context ratio 0.25" depends on a hyperparameter nobody reports next to it.
Chapters 4–6

Watch it collapse

Four models trained live with hand-written gradients, identical except for the target branch. One reaches a loss a thousand times lower than the rest and learns nothing. Second tab: the phase portrait that explains why.
Chapters 3 and 9

Inside the encoder

Draw something and step through every operation: patchify, the fixed sin-cos position table, positional similarity, attention with a content-versus-position slider, and exactly what masking removes.
Chapter 13

Planning in latent space

V-JEPA 2's planner in miniature: frozen encoder, action-conditioned predictor learned from random play, Cross-Entropy Method minimising ℓ₁ distance to a goal embedding. Break it with a horizon of one.

Suggested order

  1. Read Chapters 1–2 of the PDF, then open Multi-block masking and get a feel for what the two encoders receive.
  2. Open Watch it collapse and run it before reading Chapter 5. The theory is much easier once you have seen a loss go to zero while a representation dies.
  3. Read Chapter 5, then come back to the second tab of the same page and check the p*₋ = 0.05445 prediction yourself.
  4. Read Chapters 7–10 alongside Inside the encoder and the PyTorch code in ../code/.
  5. After Chapter 13, open Planning in latent space and try to make it fail.
What these are and are not These pages use real algorithms on toy data. The collapse simulator trains actual networks with actual gradients, and the phase portrait integrates the actual published equations. But the models are thousands of times smaller than the ones in the papers, there is no attention in the collapse demo, and the ViT weights in the encoder explorer are random rather than trained. They are for building intuition. The PyTorch code in ../code/ is for checking whether the intuition survives.
