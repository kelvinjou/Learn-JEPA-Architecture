Inside the encoder

Input

Draw with the mouse or a finger. Every panel updates live.

Controls

Powers of two from 8 to 128 px. ViT-B/16 on 224 px input gives a 14×14 grid; our CIFAR preset gives 8×8.
Mix the query/key projections between patch content and the positional embedding. At 0 the head attends purely on position; at 1 purely on content.

The pipeline

  1. Patchify. One strided convolution, kernel = stride = P. Equivalent to flattening each patch and applying one shared linear map.
  2. Add position. A fixed, non-learned 2-D sin-cos table. requires_grad=False. Half the channels encode the row, half the column.
  3. Mask. Simply drop the tokens you do not want. Attention is set-valued, so this costs nothing — and it is why the I-JEPA context encoder is cheap.
  4. Attend. Full bidirectional self-attention. No causal mask anywhere in I-JEPA, unlike your LLM intuition.
  5. Pool. No [CLS] token: average the patch tokens.

3 · its positional embedding

Blue half encodes the row, green half the column. Within each half: [sin(pos·ω) | cos(pos·ω)] with ωk = 10000−2k/(D/2).

4 · positional similarity to every patch

Cosine similarity between the selected patch's position code and every other. The bright cross is the point: sin-cos positions are separable in row and column, so a token knows where it is before it knows anything about content.

5 · attention from the selected patch

One randomly-initialised head, softmax(QKᵀ/√d), row of the selected query. Randomly-initialised — no training — so this shows the inductive bias, not learned structure.

The experiment worth running Drag the content/position slider to 0 and look at panel 5. A randomly-initialised head attending purely on position produces the same cross pattern as panel 4 — that structure is free, it comes from the sin-cos table, and no training was needed for it. Now drag to 1: attention becomes content-driven and much noisier. Almost all of what a trained ViT learns is how to blend those two.
Trap The order of operations in step 2 matters and is easy to get backwards. I-JEPA adds the positional embedding before masking, so a surviving context token still knows its absolute location in the full image. Add it after and every context token thinks it is at position 0..n_ctx, which destroys the whole task. Turn on masking and note that the boxes in panel 6 stay where they are in the original image, not where they are in the token sequence.
Try this Set the patch size to its smallest value, 8 px. The grid becomes 32×32 = 1024 tokens and the attention matrix has over a million entries — this is why ViTs use 14 or 16 pixel patches, and why V-JEPA's tubelets span two frames rather than one. Then set it to 128 px: four tokens, and the model can no longer represent anything spatial at all.
